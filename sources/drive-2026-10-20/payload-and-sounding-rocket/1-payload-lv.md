1. The WADIS / ECOMA Mesospheric Aerosol &amp; Turbulence Suite

- The mesosphere and lower thermosphere, specifically between 70 km and 120 km (active measurements from 60 km to 90–95 km).
- In a single payload, it covers atmospheric composition (CONE, Pirani), metallic ions/electrons (ion/electron probes, CPP) and aerosols/particulates (ECOMA detector, photometer, MAGIC). The presence of the MAGIC sampler proves the scientific need for a reusable payload with a parachute system to return samples to the laboratory.
- All instruments described have flown successfully across multiple suborbital sounding rocket campaigns from Andøya Rocket Range (Norway) with TRL 8–9
- The scientific instrument stack (ECOMA detector, MAGIC, CONE, CPP, booms, electronics, and internal harness) weighs approximately 70 to 95 kg and it has a modular cylindrical skin of 356 mm diameter and around 2.7 m length.

2. The MAGIC meteoric smoke particle sampler

- Sounding rocket flights achieved apogees of 107.7 km, 123.1 km, 125.4 km, 126.0 km and 130.6 km (see table 3).
- Flown and recovered across 7 suborbital sounding rocket campaigns (Esrange, Wallops Island, Andøya).
- The MAGIC unit is compact around 500mm long and it fits a 355-431mm deck. No info given for total mass.

3. [MORABA](https://moraba.de/)

- It has a standard payload outer diameter is 356 mm or 438 mm. It was built in modular cylindrical sections that bolt together using standard ring joints and designed with a front sensor deck and aft sensor deck for scientific instruments.
- But MORABA vehicles (Improved Orion, VSB-30, Red Kite) are traditionally ground launched from rails with spin stabilizing fins but the guideline may be useful anyway.
